# Website Ad Landing Page Advice

Extracted from the transcript section on website, Google ads, Facebook ads, and funnel conversion optimization.

## Core Problem

The current ads are sending traffic to a general website/homepage instead of a dedicated conversion page.

Alex's main point: every extra step in the funnel can cut conversions dramatically. If a visitor lands on a page and then has to click another button to reach the real booking or estimate flow, that is like putting two landing pages in a row.

## Main Recommendation

Create a dedicated ad landing page.

This page should be built for one purpose: converting paid traffic into booked inspections or calls.

It does not need to be part of the normal site navigation. It can be a standalone page used only for ads, and possibly for all main CTA buttons across the website.

## Recommended Landing Page Structure

1. Simple header
   - Keep the logo small.
   - Keep the top bar compact.
   - Remove unnecessary navigation links.
   - On mobile, make sure the header stays on one line.
   - Include the phone number in the top area for visitors who want to call.

2. Clear headline above the fold
   - The first screen should immediately communicate the offer.
   - Avoid using the most valuable screen space for blank space, oversized branding, or messy navigation.

3. Lead with the offer
   - Use the stronger offer language: `Free 18 Point Inspection`.
   - This performed better than generic wording like `free estimate`.
   - Make the offer obvious before the user scrolls.

4. Put the form directly under the offer
   - Do not make visitors click through to another page before submitting.
   - The form should be visible early on the page.
   - Keep the page focused on booking the inspection.

5. Include service locations
   - Add locations or service areas because visitors often want to confirm that the company serves their area.

6. Add only essential FAQs
   - Include answers to the most common objections or questions.
   - Keep this near the bottom.
   - Do not overload the page with general website content.

## What To Remove Or Reduce

- Large empty space at the top of the page.
- Oversized logo or branding block.
- Extra navigation links that distract from the conversion goal.
- Multiple CTAs leading to different actions.
- A separate button click before the visitor can submit their information.
- Repeated or generic marketing copy that does not help the visitor book.

## Button Redirect Advice

Once the new ad landing page exists, redirect the main CTA buttons on the website to this page.

This could improve conversions from both:

- Paid ad traffic.
- Organic/SEO traffic already visiting the website.

## Google Ads Advice Related To The Landing Page

The business already has strong ad economics, but the page is under-optimized.

Alex suggested that improving the funnel could potentially double the return from Google ads because the current landing experience is losing traffic.

Recommended order:

1. Fix the landing page.
2. Improve the ads.
3. Increase ad spend only after the funnel and ads are improved.

## Facebook Ad Creative Advice

The Facebook ad image needs to be readable on a phone, not just on a desktop while designing it.

Key advice:

- Text the ad image to yourself and view it at phone size.
- If the copy cannot be read on a phone, simplify it.
- Do not rely on small text, tiny checklists, or tiny phone numbers in the image.
- Lead with the offer first.
- Make `Free 18 Point Inspection` large and readable.
- Use less text overall.
- Put location language like `Hilton Head` or `Bluffton` in the ad copy or headline.
- Do not over-prioritize the logo in direct response ads.
- Use images that clearly show the work being done.
- Test pictures with happy customers near their HVAC unit.

## Ad Testing Advice

Run many creative variations instead of only a few.

Examples:

- White background.
- Blue background.
- Blue text on white.
- White text on blue.
- Different customer photos.
- Different work-in-progress photos.
- Different offer layouts.

The goal is to let the market show which combination gets the best response.

## Organic Content As Ad Creative

Use the best-performing organic posts as paid ads.

Recommended process:

1. Find organic posts that already performed well.
2. Add a short five-second CTA at the end.
3. CTA example: "If you want a free 18 point inspection, click the link and we will come straight to your house."
4. Turn those videos into ads.

The logic: organic performance is a cheap way to test which creative already gets attention.

## Priority Checklist

- Build a dedicated ad landing page.
- Make the header smaller and cleaner.
- Put phone number in the header.
- Lead with `Free 18 Point Inspection`.
- Put the form directly under the offer.
- Add service areas.
- Add essential FAQs.
- Redirect website CTA buttons to the landing page.
- Simplify Facebook ad images for mobile readability.
- Test many ad variations.
- Use top organic posts as ads with a five-second CTA.
- Increase ad spend after improving the funnel and creative.
