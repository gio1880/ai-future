1
Set up your drive base
Every program starts here. Get this block right once, and every other mission gets easier.
SetupDriveBase

Pybricks needs to know three things about your robot: which ports the drive motors use, how big your wheels are, and how far apart they sit. Measure your robot and fix the numbers below — wrong numbers here make every distance and turn wrong everywhere.

  • wheel_diameter — measure across the wheel in millimeters (small SPIKE wheels ≈ 56 mm)
  • axle_track — distance between the centers of the left and right wheels, in mm
drive_base_setup.py
from pybricks.hubs import PrimeHub
from pybricks.pupdevices import Motor
from pybricks.parameters import Port, Direction
from pybricks.robotics import DriveBase

hub = PrimeHub()

# Left motor spins backward so both wheels push the robot forward.
left = Motor(Port.A, Direction.COUNTERCLOCKWISE)
right = Motor(Port.B)

# MEASURE YOUR ROBOT and fix these two numbers!
robot = DriveBase(left, right, wheel_diameter=56, axle_track=114)

# Use the gyro for straighter driving and sharper turns.
robot.use_gyro(True)
✅ Check before moving on

Run robot.straight(500) and measure with a ruler. If your robot drives more or less than 500 mm, your wheel_diameter is wrong. Fix it until the real distance matches.

Launch & drive straight to the bridge
From the bottom-left launch zone, drive north to the river bridge in the middle of the field.
straight()Launching

Look at the map: the white launch zone is the bottom-left corner, and the red bridges cross the river near the center. A good launch is repeatable: same spot, same angle, every time. Use the lines on the launch zone border as your alignment guide.

mission_bridge_run.py
# (Paste your drive base setup from Mission 1 above this line)

# Drive from the launch zone toward the bridge.
# Measure the real distance on your mat with a tape measure!
robot.settings(straight_speed=300)   # mm per second — start slow
robot.straight(700)                  # drive 700 mm forward

hub.speaker.beep()                   # celebrate at the bridge!

robot.straight(-700)                 # reverse back home
💡 Why slow? At 300 mm/s your robot is more accurate. Once the mission works 5 times in a row, raise the speed and see if it still works — that's how competition teams tune speed vs. reliability.
🏆 Challenge: The Bridge Inspector
  • Stop with your robot's front wheels exactly on the bridge — within 2 cm — 4 out of 5 tries
  • Then return fully inside the launch zone without touching it with your hands
3
Gyro turns — reach the orchard
The orchard (the neat grid of trees at the farm) isn't straight ahead — you'll need to drive, turn, and drive again.
turn()Gyro

Real missions are never a straight line. To reach the orchard in the center of the map, you drive out of the launch zone, turn toward the farm, and approach. With use_gyro(True), the robot uses its gyro sensor to make turns accurate.

mission_orchard.py
# (Drive base setup from Mission 1 goes above)

robot.straight(400)     # leave the launch zone
robot.turn(45)          # turn right 45 degrees toward the farm
robot.straight(450)     # drive toward the orchard
robot.turn(-45)         # negative = turn left, straighten out
robot.straight(200)     # final approach

# Now drive the whole path in reverse to get home:
robot.straight(-200)
robot.turn(45)
robot.straight(-450)
robot.turn(-45)
robot.straight(-400)
💡 Gyro check: if your turns are consistently a few degrees off, run the Hub Check assignment's drift test again — you may have a drifting gyro, or you may be launching the robot before the gyro finishes calibrating (keep the robot still for a second after starting the program).
🏆 Challenge: Orchard Express
  • Reach the orchard and come ALL the way back into the launch zone, hands-free
  • Level 2: do it with only ONE turn each way (plan a smarter path!)
4
Find the black path with your color sensor
The map has three thick black paths. Instead of guessing distances, drive until your sensor SEES the line — that's how pro teams stay accurate.
ColorSensorreflection()

Dead-reckoning (driving fixed distances) drifts over long runs. The black paths on the mat are landmarks: drive until the color sensor reads dark, and you know exactly where you are — no matter how sloppy the launch was.

Mount a color sensor facing straight down, about 1 LEGO brick above the mat.

mission_find_path.py
from pybricks.pupdevices import ColorSensor
from pybricks.parameters import Port

# (Drive base setup from Mission 1 goes above)
eye = ColorSensor(Port.C)

# First: find YOUR numbers. Put the sensor over the green mat,
# then over the black path, and read the values:
print("Reflection right now:", eye.reflection())

# Usually: black path < 20, green field > 40. Pick a threshold between them.
BLACK = 25

robot.drive(150, 0)                 # start rolling forward slowly
while eye.reflection() > BLACK:     # keep going until we see the path
    pass
robot.stop()                        # found it — stop right on the line!

hub.speaker.beep()
🏆 Challenge: Path Hunter
  • Launch from three DIFFERENT spots in the launch zone — your robot should stop on the black path every single time
  • Level 2: after finding the path, turn and drive along it for 300 mm
5
Square up on a line
Use TWO color sensors to make your robot perfectly perpendicular to a black path — fixing any crooked driving instantly.
2 sensorsPrecision

If your robot approaches a line slightly crooked, one sensor crosses the line before the other. Line squaring stops each side of the robot when its own sensor sees the line — so the robot straightens itself out. Teams use this before precision missions.

mission_line_square.py
# Needs two color sensors: left on Port C, right on Port D,
# both facing down at the front of the robot.
left_eye = ColorSensor(Port.C)
right_eye = ColorSensor(Port.D)
BLACK = 25

# Creep forward with each motor until ITS sensor finds the line.
left.run(100)
right.run(100)

left_done = False
right_done = False
while not (left_done and right_done):
    if left_eye.reflection() < BLACK and not left_done:
        left.hold()
        left_done = True
    if right_eye.reflection() < BLACK and not right_done:
        right.hold()
        right_done = True

hub.speaker.beep()   # perfectly square on the line!
💡 After squaring, the gyro heading may not match reality anymore. Call hub.imu.reset_heading(0) right after squaring so future gyro turns start from a known angle.
🏆 Challenge: Crooked Launch, Straight Finish
  • Launch your robot 10–15° crooked ON PURPOSE. After line-squaring, it should end up perfectly perpendicular to the path — every time
6
The full run: Launch → Bridge → Home
Put it all together in one program with functions — the way real competition code is organized.
FunctionsFull mission

Competition programs are built from small named functions — one per mission or maneuver. That way you can test each piece alone, reorder your run, and recover fast when something changes. Here's a full run combining everything you practiced:

full_run.py
# (Drive base + sensor setup from Missions 1 and 4 go above)

def leave_launch_zone():
    robot.straight(300)

def find_the_path():
    robot.drive(150, 0)
    while eye.reflection() > BLACK:
        pass
    robot.stop()
    hub.imu.reset_heading(0)      # we know exactly where we are now

def visit_the_bridge():
    robot.turn(30)
    robot.straight(350)
    hub.speaker.beep()            # mission complete sound

def go_home():
    robot.straight(-350)
    robot.turn(-30)
    robot.straight(-550)          # all the way back into the launch zone

# The run — read it like a checklist:
leave_launch_zone()
find_the_path()
visit_the_bridge()
go_home()
🏆 Final Challenge: Competition Ready
  • Your full run works 4 out of 5 consecutive tries (that's the Robot Ready Check standard!)
  • Time it — under 30 seconds earns bragging rights
  • Swap two functions in the run order. If your code still works, you built it right
