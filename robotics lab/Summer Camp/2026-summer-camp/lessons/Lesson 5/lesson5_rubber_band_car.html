Today's Goal

Lesson Goal

Today I will use elastic energy, axles, wheels, friction, and fair testing to build a rubber band car that travels far.

Concept

Stretch, Store, Release

Stretch

Twisting or pulling the rubber band changes its shape.

Store

The rubber band holds elastic potential energy.

Release

Energy turns the axle and moves the car forward.

LEGO Building Tips

Technic Tips: Rubber Band Cars

Anchor Firmly

Connect the rubber band to a strong beam or axle so it does not slip off.

Keep Axles Parallel

Front and back axles should line up straight so the car does not drag.

Leave Space

Make sure the rubber band can wind and unwind without rubbing the frame.

Builder advice: If the car jumps, skids, or stops early, check the axle friction before adding more rubber band twists.
Build Check

Before You Race

Straight Axles

Wheels should spin freely without wobbling.

Good Grip

Wheels need traction so stored energy becomes motion.

Light Frame

A strong but light car is easier to move.

BioGlow Mission

BioGlow Mission: Elastic Rover Run

The BioGlow team needs a small rover that can cross the habitat without batteries. Engineers must store energy in a rubber band, release it smoothly, and send the rover as far as possible.

Mission prompt: How can your team get more distance without making the rover twist, skid, or stop early?
Challenge

Challenge Rules: Farthest Rubber Band Car

1Build one rubber band powered car.
2Start behind the launch line.
3Release without pushing the car.
4Measure from the line to where the car stops.
5Each team gets 3 official trials.
6Record the best distance.
Fair Testing

Keep the Test Fair

Same Start

Use the same launch line every time.

Same Floor

Test on the same surface for all teams.

One Change

Change one design feature before retesting.

Improve

Improve Your Design

Try Changing

  • Number of rubber band twists
  • Wheel size or grip
  • Car weight
  • Axle friction

Keep Track

  • Trial 1 distance
  • Design change
  • Trial 2 distance
  • What improved or got worse?
Reflection

After Testing: Reflection

1. What changed distance the most?
2. Did more twists always help? Why or why not?
3. Where did friction help, and where did it hurt?
4. What would you change next?
