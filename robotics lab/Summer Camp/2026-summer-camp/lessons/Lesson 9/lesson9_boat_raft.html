Today's Goal

Lesson Goal

Today I will use buoyancy, balance, low center of mass, and stable platform design to build a boat or raft that carries a load.

Concept

What Helps a Raft Float?

Buoyancy

A floating build pushes water away and gets upward support.

Balance

Weight should be spread evenly so the raft stays level.

Low Center

Heavy parts should stay low to reduce tipping.

LEGO Building Tips

Technic Tips: Boat / Raft Builds

Build Wide

A wider raft base is harder to tip than a narrow one.

Balance the Load

Place the load near the center, not on one edge.

Secure Parts

Attach motors, bricks, or cargo low and firmly so they do not slide.

Builder advice: Test stability with small loads before adding heavier pieces.
Build Check

Stable Before Speed

Level Float

The raft should sit flat instead of leaning.

No Loose Cargo

The load should stay attached or centered while floating.

Strong Frame

Beams should not separate when the raft is picked up.

BioGlow Mission

BioGlow Mission: Cross the Glow Lagoon

The BioGlow team needs a water-safe transport raft to move supplies across a glowing lagoon. Engineers must keep the raft level, stable, and strong enough to carry cargo.

Mission prompt: How can your team carry more supplies without making the raft tip?
Challenge

Challenge Rules: Floating Supply Raft

1Build a raft or boat with LEGO Technic pieces.
2The raft must float level for 10 seconds.
3Add one small classroom load.
4The load must stay on the raft.
5Do not hold the raft during the test.
6Record whether it floats, tips, or sinks.
Fair Testing

Keep the Test Fair

Same Water Area

Use the same tub or test zone.

Same Load

Test with the same object for each team.

Same Time

Count the same 10-second float test.

Improve

Improve Your Raft

Try Changing

  • Base width
  • Load position
  • Frame bracing
  • Where heavy parts sit

Watch For

  • Tipping to one side
  • Parts separating
  • Load sliding
  • Top-heavy design
Reflection

After Testing: Reflection

1. What helped your raft float level?
2. Where did the load need to sit?
3. What made the raft unstable?
4. What would you change before carrying a heavier load?
